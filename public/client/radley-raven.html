FAQ: What advice would you give a buyer trying to purchase a home?
Know your numbers before you start looking. Get fully pre-approved — not pre-qualified — so when you find the right property, you can move fast. In competitive San Diego markets like Carmel Valley, Carlsbad, and La Jolla, the best homes go quickly. Work with an agent who knows the neighborhoods at the street level, not just the zip code. And don't just buy a house — buy a location and a lifestyle you'll grow into.
FAQ: What advice would you give a seller trying to sell their home?
Pricing strategy is everything. The biggest mistake sellers make is overpricing and chasing the market down. My listings sell in a median 22 days — 39% faster than the Carmel Valley market average — because we price accurately from day one. Invest in professional staging and photography, declutter ruthlessly, and trust the data. A well-prepared home priced right will outperform an overpriced home every time, even in a strong market.
FAQ: What types of clients have you worked with?
First-time homebuyers, move-up families, luxury estate sellers, investors building rental portfolios, and clients relocating to San Diego from LA, the Bay Area, and out of state. I also work with sellers in Rancho Santa Fe, La Jolla, and Carlsbad's most competitive luxury neighborhoods. My sweet spot is clients who value strategy and results over hand-holding — people who want an agent who brings data, market knowledge, and a plan.
FAQ: Describe a recent transaction you are fond of.
I recently represented the buyer on a $6.2M estate on Emerald Lane in Rancho Santa Fe. The property was listed at $6.95M and had been on the market for over 100 days. We identified the seller's motivation, structured a clean offer with favorable terms, and negotiated the price down $750K while keeping the deal smooth for both sides. Closed on time, no drama. That's the kind of transaction I love — where strategy and patience create real value for the client.

I got into real estate because I've always been drawn to building things — and real estate is the intersection of design, negotiation, investment, and people.
